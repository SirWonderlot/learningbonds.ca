Step 1 · Home page (locked)

They land on LearningBonds.ca

  1. Canada Learning Bond: "Receive up to $2,000 in free deposits from the Federal Government of Canada. There are no fees to use this service." (no button)
  2. What is the Canada Learning Bond? Free money for qualified Canadians saving for their child's future education. No fees to apply.
  3. Money waiting to be claimed: over 3 million eligible children have not yet received it.
  4. The program: 6 highlights, then a small Check My Eligibility button.
  5. The process: Check eligibility, Learn your child's entitlement amount, Apply for the Learning Bond, then a small Check My Eligibility button.

The menu also has Check My Eligibility. Menu links to the Education Savings Grant, FAQ, Contact, Privacy and Terms pages lead nowhere yet. Pages not built

↓Check My Eligibility
Step 2 · The form (the only form on the site)

Eligibility questions, all on one page

Address: /eligibility/. Nothing is sent or stored. First names stay on their phone.

Each question has a short line saying why we ask.

  1. Which province or territory do you live in? Drop-down.
  2. How many children under 18 do you have? Drop-down: None, 1 to 16.
  3. Do you have any children aged 18, 19 or 20? Yes / No. If Yes: how many?
  4. Tell us about each child. "First name only. For illustration purposes only. Your child's name is not saved or shared." One row per child:
    • First name (typed)
    • Age (under 18: Under 1 to 17; then 18, 19 or 20)
    • Gender: M or F
  5. What is your family net income? Only if there are children under 18. Two buttons showing that family's limit, for example "$58,523 or less" / "More than $58,523" (2026–27 limits, by number of children under 18).
  6. For each child aged 18 to 20: "While Sam was 15 or younger, was your family net income at or below the limit in at least one year?" with that child's range of yearly limits (for example $36,378 to $50,197 at age 19). Yes / No / Not sure.
  7. Does your child currently have a Registered Education Savings Plan (RESP)? Each child listed by name, with Yes / No beside it.
  8. View my unclaimed amounts (turns on once everything is answered).

If there are no children under 21, the form says "The Canada Learning Bond is available to eligible children and young adults under the age of 21."

Step 3 · Results

Each child's results, one after another

↓
Income within the limit

"Tony could collect up to $2,000"

  • Today (including past years)
  • Going forward: $100 a year until age 15
  • B.C. Training and Education Savings Grant: $1,200 (B.C. children aged 6 to 8)
  • Total

Chart for each child under 18

What it could be worth at 18: Bond deposits, then 6%, 8% and 10% a year. Each child has their own colour (blue, purple, amber, rose). The bars grow and the amounts count up.

Notes under the charts

  • Grows at these average yearly rates; for illustration only.
  • "This is free money from the federal government. It is already there for your child. You just need to claim it."
  • "These amounts are estimates provided for illustration purposes. The actual amount may vary depending on your family's net income during each year of eligibility."
  • If a child has an RESP: some may already have been paid.
Income above the limit

"Your family net income exceeds the qualifications for the Canada Learning Bond."

Each child: "Not eligible for the Canada Learning Bond."

Exception: a B.C. child aged 6 to 8 still sees the $1,200 B.C. grant (no income limit).

↓

What is an RESP?

Short explanation: the CESG adds 20% to deposits (up to $500 a year, $7,200 in total); deposits are returned if the child does not continue their education.

Each child under 16 with no RESP: a chart with three stacked bars ($50, $100, $150 a month): your deposits, CESG, growth at 6%, and the total at 18.

Child with an RESP: "✓ Already has an RESP."

Child aged 16 or 17: a note that they can no longer receive the CESG.

Child aged 18 to 20

Yes: "Sam could collect up to $2,000" (Today: based on the years before Sam turned 16).

Not sure: "Sam may be able to collect up to $2,000".

Note: Sam requests the Learning Bond themselves, before they turn 21.

No: "Not eligible for the Canada Learning Bond."

Step 4 · One more question (every family, any income)

"Are you interested in information about additional programs that may be helpful for your child's academic and/or financial future?"

Yes / No

↓No

Nothing opens. They carry on to "How to apply" below.

↓Yes: two programs open underneath

Learning Access Grant

For children in grades 4 to 12. Up to $1,200 a year toward approved tutoring programs.

Learn more → the Learning Access Grant's own site, in a new tab. Link to come

Advantage Plus

For children aged 18 and under. A program designed to help them financially in the future. It can be used for several different things.

For each child aged 0 to 18, an example at $50, $75 and $100 a month: fully paid after 20 years, projected cash value at 65, projected death benefit at 65 (from your Advantage Plus calculator's iA figures).

Protection note, then "For illustration purposes only. Projected values are not guaranteed."

Book a call → your booking calendar. This is the only place to book a call; the specialist also helps with the Bond. Calendly link to come
