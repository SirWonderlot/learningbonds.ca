By the numbers

The program

In 2004, the Government of Canada introduced the Canada Learning Bond to help parents save for their children's future education. Some highlights of the Learning Bond program include:

  • Amount: the Learning Bond is a benefit of up to $2,000 for each eligible child.
  • Extra $25: with the first $500 payment, the government also pays $25. This is in addition to the Learning Bond payments.
  • Retroactive: the Learning Bond is a retroactive benefit, meaning you may collect for past years.
  • Total payments: over 2.3 million children have collected the Canada Learning Bond, totalling more than $2.4 billion in payments.
  • Unclaimed amounts: over 3 million eligible children are not collecting the Bond because they have not yet applied.
  • Fees: there are no fees to apply for and receive the Learning Bond. It is a free program.

A person must be eligible to collect the Canada Learning Bond.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

Is there a cost to apply?

No. There are no fees to apply for the Canada Learning Bond.

How much can my child receive?

Up to $2,000: $500 for the first year, then $100 for each year your child qualifies, up to age 15. With the first $500 payment, the government also pays $25.

Can I collect for past years?

Yes. The Learning Bond is a retroactive benefit, so your child may receive amounts for past years they qualified for.

Where does the money go?

It is deposited into a special registered account for your child, to be used when they enrol in education after high school.

My child is 18 or older. Is it too late?

Young adults aged 18 to 20 can apply for the Canada Learning Bond themselves until their 21st birthday.
